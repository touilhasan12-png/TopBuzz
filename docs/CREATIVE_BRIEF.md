# Creative brief

## Working title
**Istanbul: The City Between Two Continents**

## Promise to the viewer
In seven minutes, show how geography, imperial decisions, conflict, architecture and modern infrastructure repeatedly remade a city positioned between Europe and Asia.

## Format and audience
- English narration and on-screen English titles.
- Approximately 7 minutes; 16:9; cinematic 3D diorama / aerial time-lapse.
- Broad general audience; informed and vivid, never a lecture or a tourist advert.

## Visual identity
- A single recognizable Bosporus / Golden Horn geography anchors the sequence.
- Era palettes evolve gradually: cool limestone and deep water for the ancient city; warm stone and copper for Constantinople; restrained gold/teal for Ottoman scenes; cooler, denser lights for the modern metropolis.
- Camera language: slow establishing aerials, measured push-ins on landmarks, restrained orbiting, and clear transitions through map/time layers.
- Stylized geometry is acceptable; historic reconstructions are explicitly illustrative, not photoreal archival evidence.
- Avoid invented population counters or unsupported statistics. Any quantitative chart needs a source and a clearly labeled date/definition.

## Narrative principles
1. Geography first: explain the strait and natural harbor before listing rulers.
2. Distinguish Byzantium, Constantinople, and Istanbul as changing names/identities of the same urban site, while avoiding claims that one name instantly replaced all others.
3. Treat 1204 and 1453 as distinct turning points.
4. Show architectural continuity and transformation without implying that every building survived unchanged.
5. Correctly note that the Turkish Republic moved its capital to Ankara in 1923; Istanbul remained the country's largest historic metropolis, not the capital.
6. Include a brief sources/credits end card.
